# Consequences, 1947–2026

*Third in the series. `radcliffe-partition-analysis.md` covers what happened;
`counterfactual-maximalist-india.md` draws the alternate map. This traces what that map does to
the world over seventy-nine years.*

Confidence is marked throughout — **[H]** follows almost necessarily from the premise, **[M]** is
plausible but contested, **[L]** is a speculative chain worth stating but not betting on. The
interesting claims are mostly [M], and §8 argues the whole thing probably fails.

---

Using the **sane maximum** from the previous document — no partition, Sulaiman–Kirthar crest in
the west (Afghanistan *not* annexed), Tibet and the Kunlun in the north, Burma and the
Salween–Mekong divide in the east, Ceylon and the Bay as an interior sea.

## 2. The minerals

You asked for this explicitly. The holdings are extraordinary, and they are concentrated in
exactly the inputs that turn out to matter in the 2000s–2020s.

### 2.1 The one that matters most — Kachin heavy rare earths

Northern Kachin State is the planet's largest source of **dysprosium and terbium**. Estimates run
to **two-thirds of global heavy rare earth feedstock** originating in Myanmar, and by 2023
Chinese imports of heavy REE oxides from Myanmar reached ~41,700 tonnes — **more than double
China's own annual mining quota** for those elements.

Heavy REEs are the genuine chokepoint of the energy transition: dysprosium and terbium are what
keep neodymium magnets working at temperature, which means EV traction motors, wind turbine
generators, and precision-guided munitions. China holds ~90%+ of separation capacity — but a
large share of the *feedstock* is Burmese.

**In this timeline that feedstock is Indian.** See §6.4 for what that does to the 21st century.

### 2.2 The rest of the inventory

| Source | Holding |
|---|---|
| **Burma** | Tin (top-3 global); **jade** (Hpakant — world near-monopoly on jadeite); **rubies** (Mogok — the finest known); offshore gas (Yadana, Shwe, Zawtika); copper (Monywa); teak; enormous Irrawaddy/Salween hydro |
| **Tibet** | **Lithium** brines (Zabuye); major copper (Yulong, Qulong); chromite; geothermal; and the **water of half of Asia** |
| **Balochistan / Sindh** | **Reko Diq** — ~5.9 Bt ore at 0.41% Cu, ~41.5 Moz gold, one of the world's largest undeveloped copper-gold deposits; Saindak; **Thar lignite** (~175 Bt); Sui gas; Khewra salt; **Gwadar** |
| **Ceylon** | **Vein graphite** — the world's only commercial source of it, exceptionally pure; mineral sands (ilmenite, rutile, zircon); sapphires; **Trincomalee**, among the finest natural deep-water harbours on earth |
| **Bengal** | Bibiyana and Titas gas |
| **Nepal** | Theoretical hydro potential in the tens of GW |
| **India proper** | Coal, iron ore, bauxite, manganese, and the **world's largest thorium reserves** (Kerala monazite sands) |

### 2.3 The synthesis

Put in one column: **heavy rare earths, vein graphite, lithium, tin, copper, thorium, and the
headwaters.** That is close to the complete mineral basis of the energy transition and the
electronics supply chain inside a single border — anode material, magnet material, battery
material, conductor material, and a nuclear fuel cycle.

The actual 2020s are shaped by China's ability to use rare earths as leverage. **In this
timeline that leverage is substantially Indian, or at minimum contested rather than monopolised.**

## 3. The founding, 1947–1950

**No Direct Action Day.** [H] The 16 August 1946 Calcutta killings and the Noakhali–Bihar
retaliation chain were fought *over* partition. Remove the prize and the specific sequence goes.

**But not a peaceful founding.** [H] The Muslim League doesn't evaporate because Bose disapproves
of it. What replaces the massacre is **state coercion**: martial law in Punjab and Bengal, the
League suppressed as a secessionist organisation, its leadership detained. Bose was hostile to
the Mahasabha and the League alike, so the repression falls on both — but a hypernationalist
state suppressing a movement representing ~30% of the population is not a gentle event. Fewer
deaths than 1947 by an order of magnitude, almost certainly. Zero, no.

**The ledger, though, is overwhelming:** 14–18 million never displaced, 200,000–2,000,000 never
killed. Nothing else in this document comes close to that as a single gain.

**Tibet, 1949–50.** [M] India moves into Tibet during the Chinese Civil War, inheriting Britain's
treaty position rather than surrendering it. The window is real but narrow.

## 4. The first great cascade: Korea

**[M — the most consequential speculative chain in this document.]**

The PLA entered Tibet in **October 1950**. Chinese People's Volunteers entered Korea in
**October 1950**. Same month, same army, opposite ends of the country.

If India is holding the Tibetan plateau, the PLA's western theatre is committed at precisely the
moment Mao must decide about Korea. A China that cannot do both, doesn't.

**Consequences if China stays out of Korea:**

- MacArthur's advance to the Yalu is not thrown back. **Korea unifies under Seoul, 1951.** [M]
- No North Korea. No Kim dynasty. No DMZ, no Panmunjom, no 1968 Pueblo, no Axe Murder Incident.
- **No North Korean nuclear programme** — the single most durable proliferation problem of the
  post-Cold War era simply never exists. [M]
- Japan's post-war trajectory shifts: the Korean War procurement boom was a major engine of
  Japanese recovery. A shorter war means a smaller boom. [M]
- China does not spend 1950–53 fighting the United States, which removes the founding wound of
  Sino-American hostility. A PRC that never fights the US in Korea might normalise decades
  earlier. [L]

One border decision in the Himalaya rewrites Northeast Asia. This is the clearest illustration of
why "unseen consequence" is the right frame.

## 5. The second great cascade: no Pakistan, no 9/11

**[M, but the strongest [M] here — the causal chain is short and every link is documented.]**

This is the answer to your point about wasted geopolitical focus, and it runs far past India.

**The chain:**

1. The anti-Soviet Afghan resistance after 1979 required a **sanctuary**. It had one: Peshawar,
   Quetta, and the tribal belt.
2. **Operation Cyclone ran through the ISI.** The CIA did not arm the mujahideen directly; it
   handed weapons and money to Pakistani intelligence, which distributed them and chose the
   recipients — favouring the most Islamist factions.
3. The **madrassa belt** along the Afghan frontier, filled with refugee children, produced the
   *talibs*. **The Taliban were made in Pakistan**, and ISI backed their 1994–96 rise.
4. The **Arab-Afghan foreign fighter networks** — the Services Bureau, Peshawar, Bin Laden's
   arrival in 1979–80 — were headquartered on Pakistani soil.
5. Al-Qaeda formed out of that network. Its later Afghan sanctuary depended on the Taliban.

**Remove Pakistan and every link breaks at once.** The territory is Indian, held by a
hypernationalist state that would never sponsor a transnational Islamist insurgency on its own
frontier, and which — unlike Pakistan — has no interest in "strategic depth."

**Therefore:**

- **The Soviets probably win in Afghanistan.** [M] Without sanctuary, external supply, or an
  organised foreign-fighter pipeline, the resistance is a containable rural insurgency. No 1989
  withdrawal, no humiliation.
- One contributing factor to Soviet collapse is removed — though the USSR's terminal problems
  were structural and economic, so this delays rather than prevents. [M]
- **No Arab-Afghan phenomenon. No al-Qaeda. No 9/11.** [M]
- **No War on Terror.** No Afghanistan 2001–21, no Guantánamo, no PATRIOT Act, no drone
  campaign, no airport security regime. The 2003 Iraq invasion had independent drivers, but
  the political conditions that enabled it don't exist. [M]
- No 26/11, no Kargil, no Pathankot, no Pulwama, no LeT or JeM at all. [H]

**And the proliferation cascade:** no Pakistani bomb means **no A.Q. Khan network.** Khan
supplied centrifuge designs and hardware to **Iran** (the P-1 and P-2 that the entire Iranian
programme was built on), **Libya** (the programme surrendered in 2003), and **North Korea** (the
uranium enrichment route). Remove Khan and:

- Iran's nuclear programme is set back by decades or never becomes a crisis. [M] No JCPOA, no
  Stuxnet, no Israeli strike planning, no 2015–26 standoff.
- North Korea — if it exists at all after §4 — loses the HEU path entirely. [M]
- Libya never has a programme to give up. [H]

**The 21st century's two defining security preoccupations — jihadist terrorism and nuclear
proliferation — are both substantially downstream of Pakistan's existence.**

## 6. The China axis, 1950–2026

This replaces India–Pakistan as the organising rivalry, and it is a far larger one.

### 6.1 The permanent war

Holding Tibet makes Sino-Indian conflict **existential and structural** rather than episodic. [H]
There is no 1962 as we know it — instead a longer, larger contest fought on the Kunlun, with
India holding the heights and the logistics running downhill *away* from it. The 1962 asymmetry
is inverted.

### 6.2 China's water hostage

The Yangtze and Yellow River headwaters lie on the Tibetan plateau. **In this timeline, Chinese
civilisation's two founding rivers rise in Indian-held territory.** [H]

This is a vulnerability without modern parallel. It would dominate Chinese grand strategy for
eighty years, and it makes a major war over the plateau likelier than not at some point in the
period. [M]

### 6.3 No maritime Belt and Road

Every port in China's "String of Pearls" is Indian here: **Gwadar, Hambantota, Chittagong,
Kyaukpyu.** [H]

- **CPEC never exists** — there is no corridor and no partner. [H]
- The maritime arm of the Belt and Road is not weakened, it is **impossible**. [H]
- With the Andamans, the Tenasserim coast to the Kra, and Ceylon, India holds the western
  approach to Malacca outright. **China's "Malacca Dilemma" becomes absolute** rather than
  theoretical. [H]

A China boxed out of the Indian Ocean, with its rivers rising in hostile territory, still
industrialises — but its strategic reach is capped in a way it never actually was.

### 6.4 The rare earth inversion

The 2010 Chinese REE embargo against Japan, and the 2020s export-control regime that followed,
rest on control of supply. **With Kachin in Indian hands, that lever is at best shared and at
worst reversed.** [H]

The 2020s trade politics of magnets, EVs, and precision munitions run through Delhi rather than
Beijing — or through neither, because a genuine duopoly is much harder to weaponise than a
monopoly. This is arguably the largest single economic consequence in the whole document. [M]

## 7. Everything else, by domain

### 7.1 The Cold War

- **No CENTO southern anchor, no US–Pakistan alliance.** [H] The U-2 that Gary Powers flew in
  1960 took off from **Peshawar**; the Badaber listening station monitored Soviet missile tests.
  American signals and overflight coverage of Soviet Central Asia is simply gone. [H]
- Washington must court or contain a 500-million-person authoritarian non-aligned giant. There
  is no client-state option. [H]
- **The Non-Aligned Movement becomes a real third bloc** rather than a debating society, because
  its leader is a genuine great power. [M]
- No 1971 US tilt, no Seventh Fleet in the Bay, and therefore no Indo-Soviet Treaty — this India
  never needs a patron. [H]

### 7.2 The Islamic world

With ~640M Muslims — about a third of the global total, more than the Arab world and Iran
combined — **this state is a first-rank Islamic power by demography whether it likes it or
not.** [H]

- Its position on Israel–Palestine carries the weight of a superpower, not a bystander. [M]
- No "Islamic bomb," no Pakistan–Saudi nuclear understanding. [H]
- Gulf labour migration still happens and is larger; remittance flows are internal-adjacent
  rather than a foreign-policy instrument. [M]

### 7.3 Economy

**The determining variable is whether Bose's state is Park Chung-hee or Ne Win** — an
authoritarian developmental state or an authoritarian extractive one. Bose chaired the Congress
National Planning Committee in 1938 and was a convinced statist, so the early decades are heavy
industry and central planning either way. The fork comes in the 1980s–90s. [M]

- **Food security transforms.** Burma was the world's largest rice exporter before the war, and
  the loss of Burmese rice in 1942 was a direct contributor to the 1943 Bengal famine. Integrated,
  Burmese rice plus undivided Punjab's wheat makes this the largest agricultural bloc on earth. [H]
- **Energy exposure collapses.** Sui, Bibiyana, Titas, Yadana, Shwe, Zawtika, plus Thar lignite,
  plus Himalayan and Irrawaddy hydro. The 1973 and 1979 oil shocks land far more softly. [H]
- 2026 GDP: **$6–9T** on the extractive path, **$18–25T+** on the developmental path. The spread
  is the whole story, and nothing in the premise determines which. [L]

### 7.4 Migration and diaspora

- Britain's "Pakistani" and "Bangladeshi" communities are simply Indian. [H] Bradford,
  Birmingham, and Tower Hamlets have the same people and an entirely different politics of
  identity — no 1971-derived Bengali diaspora nationalism, no Kashmir-derived mobilisation. [M]
- No Bangladeshi migration question in Assam, because it is internal movement. **The NRC and CAA
  have no subject matter.** [H]

### 7.5 Cricket, since you asked

The rivalry that generates the most money in the sport doesn't exist. What replaces it is worse
for everyone else:

**A combined side.** India's historic weakness was fast bowling; Pakistan's was batting depth.
Put Imran, Wasim, Waqar, Shoaib and Kapil in one attack, then bat Gavaskar, Miandad, Tendulkar,
Inzamam, Dravid, Sangakkara, Jayasuriya, Kohli and Babar in front of them, and give the spin to
**Murali** — Ceylonese, therefore Indian — with Shakib at seven.

That is not a strong team. That is a competition-ending team, and the Murali–Warne argument
becomes an internal selection matter.

**The real point is financial.** The BCCI already dominates world cricket's revenue with 1.4
billion people. At **2 billion**, cricket becomes a one-nation sport with an international
exhibition circuit attached, and the ICC is a subsidiary in all but name. Meanwhile hockey —
where India and Pakistan were *already* the two dominant nations for decades — stops being a
competition at all. [M, cheerfully]

## 8. The corrective: focus is not freed, it is redirected

Your premise is that without Pakistan, India stops wasting its attention. **I think that's the
one part of this that's wrong, and it's worth being blunt about.**

This state does not get to be relaxed. It gets to be the USSR.

**It inherits, simultaneously:**

| Insurgency | Status |
|---|---|
| Kachin, Karen, Shan, Chin, Wa | Live since 1948–49, in the world's most defensible terrain |
| Baloch | Live since 1948 |
| Pashtun / frontier | Never pacified by anyone |
| Tamil (Ceylon) | India is now the counterinsurgent, not the mediator |
| Tibetan | Post-annexation, with a living monastic society |
| Naga, Mizo, Manipuri | As actual history |
| Naxalite / Maoist | Worse — more territory, more forest, more grievance |

That is **a dozen simultaneous internal wars**, several in jungle and mountain terrain that has
defeated every army sent into it. The security establishment is not smaller than actual India's.
It is enormously larger, and it is pointed **inward**. The Pakistan problem is not deleted; it is
dissolved into the interior and multiplied.

### The structural comparison

Multinational empires held by coercion, ranked by core-nationality share:

| State | Core group | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| China | Han ~91% | Holds |
| India (actual) | Hindu ~80%, federal and democratic | Holds |
| **This India** | **Hindu ~57%, coercive integration** | **?** |
| USSR | Russian ~51% | **Dissolved 1991** |
| Yugoslavia | Serb ~36% | **Dissolved 1991–2001** |
| Austria-Hungary | German ~24% | **Dissolved 1918** |

This entity sits on the wrong side of the line, and it has an aggravating factor the USSR did
not: **Burma, Ceylon, Nepal and Tibet all had living memory of independence and mature
nationalist movements at the moment of absorption.** Aung San's AFPFL and the Ceylon National
Congress were not going to be quiet.

### The likely 1990s

**[M]** When the Soviet model fails and the ideological legitimacy of authoritarian planning
collapses worldwide, this state faces its crisis with a 57% core nationality, a dozen live
insurgencies, and peripheral nations that remember being sovereign.

The probable outcome is **partial dissolution**: Burma first — most distinct, most peripheral,
most defensible terrain — then Ceylon and Nepal, then Tibet, and possibly a Muslim-majority
west under the same logic that produced the Pakistan demand in the first place.

**Which produces the irony this whole exercise arrives at.** You may well get partition anyway —
just forty-five years later, drawn by insurgents rather than by a barrister, along lines nobody
planned, and with the Cold War's ending as its trigger rather than a British timetable.

The one thing that is *not* recoverable in that scenario, and the one thing that makes the whole
counterfactual worth taking seriously anyway, is that **the 1947 massacre still never happened.**
14 million people are not displaced and up to two million are not killed, whatever the map looks
like in 1991.

| Korea | **Possibly unified under Seoul, 1951** |
| North Korean nuclear programme | Possibly never exists |
| Soviet–Afghan War | **USSR probably wins** |

| A.Q. Khan network → Iran, Libya, DPRK | Never exists |
| CPEC and maritime Belt and Road | Impossible |
| China's Malacca Dilemma | Absolute |
| Chinese river headwaters | **Held by a hostile power** |
| Heavy rare earth leverage | **Indian or contested, not Chinese** |
| U-2 / Peshawar / Badaber SIGINT | Never available to the US |
| NRC / CAA | No subject matter |
| Hindu share of population | **80% → 57%** |
| Simultaneous internal insurgencies | **~12** |
| Survival of the state to 2026 | **Doubtful** |

**The four biggest items are all things that don't happen: the partition massacre, the Afghan
jihad and its progeny, the Khan network, and the Korean division.** The gains from what India
*acquires* — the minerals, the plateau, the ocean — are large and real, but they are second
order next to the catastrophes that never get started.

And the cost of holding it all is that the state spends eighty years looking inward at its own
provinces, which is a heavier and less glorious burden than watching one border.
