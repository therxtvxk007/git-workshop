# The 3D Questionnaire — Locating Yourself on the OCD × ADHD × Schizophrenia Space

> **Read this first.** This is a *self-reflection and educational* instrument, **not** a
> diagnostic test. No questionnaire can diagnose OCD, ADHD, or a schizophrenia-spectrum
> condition — only a qualified clinician can, through interview and history. High scores here
> mean "traits worth talking to a professional about," nothing more. If any of this is causing
> you distress, please reach out to a clinician or a crisis line.

---

## How it works

Modern psychiatry (the NIMH **RDoC** and the **HiTOP** frameworks, plus the 2025 *Nature*
cross-disorder genetics study) treats these conditions not as separate boxes but as **continuous
dimensions** that everyone sits somewhere on. So instead of "do you *have* X?", this asks
"*how far along* each axis are you?" — and places you as a single point in a 3D space:

- **Axis O — Obsessive–Compulsive** (grounded in the **OCI-R**)
- **Axis A — Attention / Hyperactivity** (grounded in **DSM-5 / ASRS-5**)
- **Axis S — Schizophrenia-spectrum / Schizotypy** (grounded in the **O-LIFE** and **CAPE-42**)

**Scoring each item:** rate how true each statement has been for you *over the last 6 months*,
in a way that is **more than most people** and **gets in the way of your life**:

| Score | Meaning |
|------:|---------|
| **0** | Not at all / never |
| **1** | A little / rarely |
| **2** | Moderately / sometimes |
| **3** | A lot / often |
| **4** | Extremely / nearly always, and it interferes |

Add up each axis (12 items × 0–4 = **0–48 raw**). The interactive tool (`index.html`) converts
each axis to a **0–100** score and plots your point. Bands per axis: **0–24 low**, **25–49
sub-threshold**, **50–74 elevated**, **75–100 high (worth a professional conversation)**.

## Axis O — Obsessive–Compulsive (12 items)

*Based on the Obsessive-Compulsive Inventory-Revised (OCI-R): washing, checking, ordering,
obsessing, hoarding, and mental neutralizing — plus core OCD features (doubt, "just-right",
inflated responsibility).*

1. **Washing / contamination** — I wash, clean, or avoid things far more than needed because of
   fears of germs, dirt, or contamination.
2. **Checking** — I repeatedly check things (locks, appliances, switches, that I didn't cause harm)
   even when I know I already checked.
3. **Ordering / symmetry** — I get very upset if objects aren't arranged, aligned, or symmetric in
   a specific way, and I re-arrange them until they feel right.
4. **Obsessing** — Unwanted, distressing thoughts, images, or urges pop into my mind and I can't
   get rid of them.
5. **Hoarding** — I collect or keep things I don't need and find it very hard to throw things away.
6. **Mental neutralizing** — I feel compelled to count, repeat words/phrases, or "cancel" a bad
   thought with a good one to prevent something bad from happening.
7. **Pathological doubt** — I need to ask for reassurance, or re-do things, because I can't trust
   my own memory or judgment.
8. **"Just-right" / incompleteness** — I repeat actions until they feel *complete* or *just right*,
   even without a logical reason.
9. **Inflated responsibility / magical prevention** — I feel that if I don't perform a certain
   ritual, I'll be responsible for something terrible happening.
10. **Time cost** — My rituals or intrusive thoughts take up a significant amount of time each day
    (e.g., an hour or more).
11. **Distress when blocked** — If I'm prevented from completing a ritual, I feel intense anxiety
    or dread.
12. **Ego-dystonic awareness** — Part of me recognizes these thoughts/behaviors are excessive or
    irrational, yet I still feel driven to do them.

## Axis A — Attention / Hyperactivity–Impulsivity (12 items)

*Based on the DSM-5 ADHD criteria and the Adult ADHD Self-Report Scale (ASRS-5): 9 inattention +
core hyperactive-impulsive items. Rate traits present since childhood and across settings.*

**Inattention**
1. **Careless errors** — I make careless mistakes or miss details in work, school, or tasks.
2. **Sustaining attention** — I have trouble keeping my attention on tasks, reading, or
   conversations (my mind wanders).
3. **Listening** — People say I don't seem to listen even when spoken to directly.
4. **Follow-through** — I start things but fail to finish them or lose track of the steps.
5. **Organization** — I struggle to organize tasks, manage time, and keep things in order.
6. **Avoiding effort** — I avoid or dread tasks that require sustained mental effort.
7. **Losing things** — I frequently lose or misplace things I need (keys, phone, paperwork).
8. **Distractibility** — I'm easily pulled off task by noises, notifications, or my own thoughts.
9. **Forgetfulness** — I forget daily obligations (appointments, chores, returning calls).

**Hyperactivity / Impulsivity**
10. **Restlessness** — I feel physically restless, fidget, or can't sit still comfortably.
11. **"Driven by a motor"** — I feel internally revved up, always on the go, or can't relax/wind down.
12. **Impulsivity** — I blurt things out, interrupt others, act before thinking, or can't wait my turn.

## Axis S — Schizophrenia-spectrum / Schizotypy (12 items)

*Based on the Oxford-Liverpool Inventory (O-LIFE) — Unusual Experiences, Cognitive Disorganization,
Introvertive Anhedonia — and the CAPE-42 positive/negative dimensions. These measure sub-clinical
"psychosis-proneness" traits, which most people have to a small degree.*

**Positive / unusual experiences**
1. **Perceptual anomalies** — I sometimes see, hear, or sense things that others don't seem to
   (shadows, sounds, presences).
2. **Ideas of reference / magical thinking** — I feel that ordinary events (songs, headlines,
   strangers) carry special hidden messages meant for me.
3. **Suspiciousness** — I often feel watched, followed, or that people are plotting against me.
4. **Unusual beliefs** — I have experiences I'd describe as telepathy, premonitions, or forces
   others would find hard to believe.

**Cognitive disorganization**
5. **Jumbled thinking** — My thoughts feel muddled, racing, or hard to hold in a clear line.
6. **Derealization / fog** — I feel detached, dreamlike, or like the world around me isn't quite real.

**Negative / introvertive anhedonia**
7. **Anhedonia** — I get little pleasure or reward from activities and relationships I "should" enjoy.
8. **Emotional flatness** — I feel emotionally numb, blunted, or distant from my own feelings.
9. **Social withdrawal** — I strongly prefer being alone and have very few close relationships by choice.

**Disorganization / negative behavior**
10. **Odd speech** — People say my speech goes off on tangents or is hard to follow.
11. **Eccentricity** — Others describe my behavior, appearance, or manner as odd or unusual.
12. **Avolition** — I struggle to start or sustain motivation for goals, work, or self-care.

## Why some items look like they belong to two axes (the overlap map)

The whole point of a 3D model is that the axes are **correlated, not independent** — the 2025
genetics data confirms real shared biology. A few items deliberately live near a boundary; that
overlap *is* the signal, and it's what distinguishes the conditions:

| Overlap zone | Looks like… | but differs because… |
|---|---|---|
| **O6/O4 mental rituals ↔ S1/S2 unusual experiences** | intrusive mental content | OCD intrusions are *unwanted and resisted* (ego-dystonic); psychotic experiences are *believed* (ego-syntonic). |
| **A2/A8 distractibility ↔ S5 cognitive disorganization** | can't hold a thought | ADHD = attention *drifts* to external pulls; schizotypy = thought *stream itself* feels jumbled/derealized. |
| **A12 impulsivity ↔ S (O-LIFE Impulsive Nonconformity)** | acting without control | ADHD impulsivity is about *inhibition/reward*; schizotypal impulsivity co-travels with eccentricity. |
| **A11 restlessness ↔ O11 ritual anxiety** | inner tension | ADHD restlessness is *undirected*; OCD tension is *relieved by a specific ritual*. |
| **S9 social withdrawal ↔ O contamination avoidance** | avoiding people | schizotypal withdrawal is *low desire for closeness*; OCD avoidance is *fear-driven*, closeness still wanted. |

The interactive tool reports each axis separately *and* flags when two axes are both elevated, so
you can see genuine co-occurrence rather than forcing yourself into one box.
