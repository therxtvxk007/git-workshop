## SLIDE 1 — AI-CASCADE: what caused the crash?

Title: AI-CASCADE
Subtitle: A layer-by-layer picture of the money behind AI

On the slide:

- A stock market crash has two different causes.
- Cause A is new information. The companies are worth less than before.
- Cause B is forced sales. Rules and machines sell shares automatically.
- Cause A is good. The market must find the correct price.
- Cause B is bad. It is a fault in the machine. It is not news about the world.
- AI-CASCADE measures how much of a crash comes from each cause.
- Then AI-CASCADE finds the smallest rule change that stops cause B.
- The rule change must not stop cause A.

Words on this slide:

- Crash: a very large decrease of prices in a short time.
- Shock: a sudden event that changes prices.
- Tomography: a method that makes a picture of the inside of a body in layers.
  A hospital scanner uses this method. We use the same idea for the market.

Speaker notes:

We cannot see the full loan network behind the fast increase in AI expenditure.
We must not pretend that we can. We use the Korean crashes of 2026 as a test
site. From these crashes, we recover the full set of possible hidden systems.
Then we find the smallest market rule that is safe for all of these systems.

Visual: One arrow that points down. The arrow has two colours. One colour is
"true news". The other colour is "forced sales". The project finds the position
of the line between the two colours.

## SLIDE 2 — The AI money chain (start from zero)

Title: How AI money moves from a data centre to a Korean share

On the slide:

- Large technology companies buy AI equipment. We call this money "capex".
- The equipment is chips, memory, chip packages, buildings and electrical power.
- Samsung and SK hynix are Korean companies. They make the memory chips.
- These companies take large loans to build new factories.
- Investors buy the shares of these companies. Large funds hold large positions.
- Because of this, one AI decision in the USA can move a Korean share price.
- The chain has many steps. The public can see only some of the steps.

Words on this slide:

- Capex: the money that a company pays for equipment and buildings.
- HBM: a fast memory chip for AI computers.
- Share: a small part of a company. A person can buy it and sell it.
- Index: a number that shows the average price of a group of shares.
- KOSPI: the primary stock market index of Korea.

Speaker notes:

Each step in this chain looks safe alone. The danger comes from the sum of the
steps. A company takes a loan for a factory. A fund also takes a loan, and buys
the shares of that company. The same risk is then in more than one place. No
public document shows this total.

Visual: A chain of five boxes from left to right. Box 1 is "AI capex". Box 5 is
"Korean share price". Draw a dotted line below the boxes. Mark the dotted line
"we cannot see this".

## SLIDE 3 — Korea in 2026: many shocks, one hidden system

Title: The first test site

On the slide (input data — check each number):

- In March, foreign investors moved the largest quantity of money out of Korea.
- Doubts about AI expenditure decreased the chip share prices many times.
- On 28 July, the KOSPI index decreased 10.84 percent in one day.
- The exchange stopped all trade for 20 minutes.
- Foreign investors sold shares with a value of 4.97 trillion won.
- Samsung decreased 13.39 percent. SK hynix decreased 14.65 percent.
- Together, the two companies were more than one half of the index.
- On 5 August, Korea stopped all new single-stock leveraged ETFs.
- Korea also increased the necessary cash deposit for these products.

Speaker notes:

Korea is the correct first test site for three reasons. First, Korea gave us
different shocks to approximately the same hidden system. Second, two companies
are more than one half of the index. Third, Korea made a real rule change on 5
August 2026. Therefore we can test our method against a real result. We do not
only make a simulation.

## SLIDE 4 — Two causes need two different answers

Title: Why the difference is important

On the slide:

- Cause A is a change of the true value. Profits, interest rates, energy prices,
  government decisions and AI demand can change the true value.
- Cause B is a mechanical effect. Five machines make cause B.
- Machine 1: a leveraged ETF must adjust its position at the end of the day.
- Machine 2: a margin loan becomes too large. The broker sells the shares.
- Machine 3: the collateral loses value. The lender asks for more collateral.
- Machine 4: the market makers stop. Liquidity becomes very low.
- Machine 5: two companies are one half of the index. All funds move together.
- If cause A is large, a halt only hides the news. The price decrease returns.
- If cause B is large, a small rule change can prevent much of the decrease.
- No system measures the two parts after a crash today.

Words on this slide:

- Leveraged ETF: a fund that moves 2 times more than the index.
- Margin loan: money from a broker. The investor buys shares with this money.
- Broker: a company that buys shares and sells shares for an investor.
- Collateral: property that a lender can take if the borrower does not pay.
- Liquidity: how easy it is to sell a large quantity at a fair price.
- Market maker: a company that always offers to buy and to sell.
- Price discovery: the process that finds the correct price from the news.
- Halt: a stop of all trade for a set time.

Speaker notes:

A regulator must know the size of the two parts before the regulator makes a new
rule. A rule against cause B is good. The same rule against cause A is bad,
because it hides true information. Our system gives the two sizes as intervals.
It does not give one exact number. A correct interval is more useful than a
false exact value.

Visual: Two columns. Column A shows "information". Column B shows the five
machines. Put a question mark between the two columns.

## SLIDE 5 — The hidden system has five layers

Title: What we must reconstruct

On the slide (a table):

| Layer | Name | What is hidden |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | AI real economy | Capex plans, HBM orders, package capacity, power contracts |
| 2 | Corporate finance | Debt, private credit, purchase agreements, guarantees, collateral |
| 3 | Market concentration | Index weights, foreign ownership, passive fund positions |
| 4 | Mechanical leverage | Leveraged ETFs, margin loans, options, futures, collateral limits |
| 5 | Market operation | The closing auction, low liquidity, circuit breakers, FX loops |

- The BIS gives a warning about this chain in its 2026 report.
- The BIS says that a large decrease of AI expenditure can move through the
  suppliers that have debt.
- The BIS says that the AI companies give money to each other in a circle.
- The BIS says that these companies do not make all of the data public.
- The BIS also says that a company can give the same property as collateral
  two times.
- But the BIS describes the danger only. The BIS does not reconstruct the
  network from a crash. The BIS does not select a rule.
- AI-CASCADE starts at this point.

Speaker notes:

The five layers are one system. They are not five separate systems. A shock in
layer 1 becomes a price decrease in layer 3. That price decrease starts the
machines in layer 4. The machines in layer 5 then make the price decrease
larger. This loop is the subject of the project.

Visual: Five horizontal layers, one above the other. Draw one arrow down through
all five layers. Draw a second arrow from layer 5 back to layer 3. Mark the
second arrow "the loop".

## SLIDE 6 — We cannot find one true network. We do not try.

Title: The identified set

On the slide:

- Many different hidden networks give the same prices and the same volumes.
- Therefore exact recovery of one true network is not possible.
- Some persons show one network diagram. Nobody can know if that diagram is
  correct.
- We calculate the set of all networks that agree with the data.
- We give this set the name H(D).

Formula:

    H(D) = { (G, θ) : (G, θ) agrees with the data D }

How to read the formula:

- D is the data: the filings, the money flows and the market records.
- G is the network of connections between the five layers.
- θ is the rule set. It contains the margin limits, the rebalance rules, the
  liquidity responses and the reactions of the investors.
- A pair (G, θ) is a member of H(D) if it can make the data that we saw.

Speaker notes:

This is a correct position, and it is also a technical advantage. If we select
one network, one error in that network makes the full result wrong. If we keep
the full set, we can ask a stronger question. We ask which conclusions are true
for every member of the set. Those conclusions are safe.

Visual: A cloud with many small network diagrams in it. Mark the cloud "H(D) —
all networks that agree with the data".

## SLIDE 7 — The new idea: decision identifiability

Title: The network is not identifiable. The decision can be identifiable.

On the slide:

- "Identifiable" means this: the data gives you one answer only.
- The hidden network is not identifiable. Many networks give the same data.
- But we do not need the true network. We need one safe rule.
- We look for one rule change I. This rule change must be safe for every member
  of H(D).

Formula:

    I* = arg min C(I)
         subject to:
         sup A(h, I) ≤ A_max         for all h in H(D)
         sup P_distortion(h, I) ≤ δ  for all h in H(D)

How to read the formula:

- I is a rule change. Example: a random rebalance window.
- C(I) is the cost of the rule change. We want the smallest cost.
- A(h, I) is the mechanical amplification. It is the extra price decrease from
  the machines.
- A_max is the largest amplification that we accept.
- P_distortion(h, I) is the damage to price discovery.
- δ is the largest damage that we accept.
- "sup ... for all h" means this: the worst result across all possible networks.

The research question:

- When do many different networks give the same safe rule?

Speaker notes:

This is the theoretical contribution. It is stronger than one more crash
forecast. A forecast is correct or wrong one time only. A decision
identifiability result tells you when you have enough data for a decision. This
question is new in this form. It also has an answer that a regulator can use.

Visual: The same cloud from slide 6. Draw one key that opens all of the small
networks in the cloud. Mark the key "I*".

## SLIDE 8 — What the system gives for each crash

Title: The five outputs

On the slide:

1. Fundamental interval.
   The part of the price decrease that comes from profits, interest rates,
   energy, government decisions or true AI demand. We give a range. We do not
   give one number.

2. Mechanical interval.
   The part that comes from ETF rebalance operations, margin sales, collateral
   calls, low liquidity and index concentration.

3. Decision-equivalent network family.
   Not one false diagram. Only the loops that every compatible network contains.

4. Minimum safe rule change (the robust intervention).
   Examples:
   - a random rebalance window;
   - a dynamic leverage limit;
   - a new closing auction rule;
   - one common halt across two markets;
   - a margin schedule.

5. No-go certificate.
   If the public data cannot support a safe rule, the system says so. Then the
   system gives the minimum additional disclosure that removes the problem.

Speaker notes:

Output 5 is as important as output 4. Many models always give an answer, also
when the data is too weak. Our system can say "no". An answer of "no" with a
list of the necessary data is a useful result for a regulator. It changes a
research limit into a request for data.

Visual: A report card with five lines. Line 5 has a red mark and the words "more
data necessary".

## SLIDE 9 — Data, the first test, and the transfer tests

Title: The first version uses public data only

Data sources (all public):

- KRX Data Marketplace: prices, trade data by investor class, foreign ownership
  and short position statistics.
- OpenDART: company filings and the original XML documents.
- KOFIA: margin loan statistics and market statistics.
- ETF documents: the prospectus, the daily positions, the shares outstanding and
  the formal rebalance rules.
- ADRs, foreign chip indexes, FX rates and public announcements with time stamps.
- Public disclosures of capex, debt and purchase agreements.

CAUTION: Order-level KRX data is not public. Do not assume free access to it. It
is a later step with the regulator or with the exchange.

The first test (we hide a real rule change from the model):

- Give only the data from before August 2026 to the model.
- Then let the model calculate the effect of the Korean rules of 5 August.
- Question 1: did the closing-price reversals decrease?
- Question 2: did the volatility move to other products?
- Question 3: did the margin stress decrease?
- Question 4: did price discovery improve, or did it only become slow?
- Question 5: were the substitution channels of the model correct?

The transfer tests:

- The 2010 Flash Crash in the USA.
- The 2015 margin unwind in China.
- The 2022 LDI forced-sale event in the United Kingdom.
- The Bank of England report of 2022 shows the connection between collateral
  calls, forced sales and contagion.

Words on this slide:

- Substitution channel: the different product that the risk moves to, after a
  new rule.
- Closing-price reversal: a price movement at the close that goes back in the
  opposite direction on the next day.

Speaker notes:

The rule change of 5 August is our best test, because it is a real experiment. A
model that cannot calculate the result of a real rule change is not good enough
for policy work. We test the model against the past first. Then we test it in
three other countries and in three other decades.

Visual: A time line with two parts. Part 1 is the data for the model. Part 2 is
the test period after 5 August. Draw a wall between the two parts.

## SLIDE 10 — What is new, what is not, and when we stop

Title: The position that we can defend

Work that exists now (these are competitors. They are not our invention):

| Work | What it gives |
|---|---|
| "Preying on Leveraged ETFs", August 2026 | The Korean closing-rebalance effect |
| Financial network reconstruction | Approximate hidden exposures, from totals |
| ECB system-wide stress tests | Second-round contagion on exposure networks |
| Robust network intervention | The largest safety limits, when the shock is not known |
| Circuit breaker research | Tests of halts with a fixed length |

What remains after we subtract all of this:

- We recover the identified set of cross-layer networks and forced-sale rules
  from more than one crash record.
- Then we make one minimum rule change from that set.
- We must show that this rule change is safe for every compatible network.
- At the same time, the rule change must keep true price discovery.
- This can be a new type of problem. We call it an inverse-decision problem.
- It is not yet proof of a new universal AI method. We say this clearly.

Stop conditions (we stop the project or we make it smaller):

- A simple combination of the tools that exist now gives the same result.
- The two intervals stay too wide, and we cannot select a policy.
- Every safe answer becomes "close the market".
- The rules decrease volatility only because they damage price discovery.
- The model cannot calculate the result of a real rule change that we hid.
- The public data cannot support the first-stage claims.

The plan and the ask:

- The Korean study is Paper 1. It is not the full project.
- The final system is a national stress twin for the AI economy. It covers
  chips, data centres, power contracts, private credit, hyperscaler agreements,
  markets and FX rates.
- In Korea, we can observe a real crash.
- In India, we can prevent the problem, before the loans for AI equipment
  become complex.
- C-DAC does the large computations, the simulations and the formal checks.
- SEBI, the RBI or an exchange makes the policy decision.
- The system stays a shadow system for the regulator. It does not buy shares
  and it does not sell shares.

Speaker notes (the summary sentence):

We cannot observe the full loan and leverage network behind the fast increase in
AI expenditure. We must not pretend that we can. I want to use repeated shocks,
such as the KOSPI crashes, to recover the full set of possible hidden systems.
Then I want to find the smallest market rule that is safe across all of them.
That rule must not stop true information.

Visual: Two maps. Map 1 is Korea, with the words "observe the crash". Map 2 is
India, with the words "prevent the crash".

# Appendix B — The Simplified Technical English rules that this deck obeys

- Use only the approved words and the Technical Names in Appendix A.
- Give one definition to each word. Use the same word for the same idea.
- Keep a descriptive sentence to a maximum of 25 words.
- Keep an instruction to a maximum of 20 words. Give one instruction per
  sentence.
- Use the active voice. Do not use the passive voice.
- Use the simple present tense, the simple past tense or the simple future tense.
- Do not use the -ing form, except in a Technical Name.
- Do not remove the article "a", "an" or "the".
- Do not make a noun cluster of more than three nouns.
- Keep a paragraph to a maximum of six sentences.
- Use "must" for a necessary action. Use "can" for a possible action.
- Put a WARNING or a CAUTION before the text that it applies to.
- Use a vertical list for complex information.
