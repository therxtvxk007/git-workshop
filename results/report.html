Permeability Tomography

Can several individually uninformative, passively observed processes identify a shared hidden interface — because each one crosses it differently? A synthetic study, run against the project's own kill criteria. Two of the three bite.

On geometry, essentially yes: a wombling baseline handed the same detector matches the joint fit everywhere except the dated frontier (0.59 vs 0.17 cells). On the permeability profile it does not — error 0.21 vs 0.13, and it calls the interface type correctly 5 times in 15 against 14.

Does the recovered interface predict a tracer it never saw?

Yes, where there is anything to predict. Held out entirely, a strongly blocked tracer gains up to +0.059 R² against +0.005 for a randomly placed curve, and its permeability comes back at 0.10 against a true 0.10. Where the held-out tracer is porous the gain is correctly ~0.

The setup

Five processes, one buried frontier, and five red herrings

Five tracers — market prices, road orientation, land tenure, tax capacity, settlement density — equilibrate across a grid under their own independent smooth forcing. One interface runs through all of them, but each tracer crosses it with a different permeability τ, and the interface exists only over a window of epochs.

The part that makes this a real problem: every tracer also carries a private discontinuity nobody else sees. Real geography is full of sharp things — rivers, scarps, soil boundaries, forest edges — and each shows up in some processes and not others. Without them the task is trivial; any one tracer's residual map hands you the answer. With them, the only thing distinguishing the frontier from a river is that the frontier recurs across tracers.

Why fusion

Each map holds two ridges. Only one of them recurs.

Fit each tracer's best smooth no-interface model and look at what sharpness survives in the residual. A smooth source basis cannot manufacture a one-cell jump, so whatever jump remains is interface-like — but it is interface-like whether it came from the shared frontier or from that tracer's own river.

This is where the multi-tracer claim earns its keep, and the number is stark. A typical single tracer misses the interface by 6 to 7.4 cells — a sixth of the domain. The joint fit misses by 0.17 to 1.54.

An oracle that picks the best tracer after the fact does nearly as well as the joint fit. But that oracle does not exist: you cannot know in advance which of your data series is the one uncontaminated by a river.

Read this honestly. Against plain areal wombling — the existing practice — the joint fit wins clearly on the dated frontier (0.17 vs 0.59) and on the late-appearing market jurisdiction (1.54 vs 5.52). But hand that same wombling baseline this method's own detector and chronology screen, and the geometry gap almost closes. Most of the geometry gain comes from the detector, not from the physics-based refinement. Geometry alone is not the contribution.

The economic column carries one hard failure (17.5 cells, seed 3) where the true interface sits at x ≈ 7.7, hard against the domain edge, while all five distractors sit centrally. Less transport crosses a near-edge interface, so it loses. Excluding it, economic recovery is 1.28 ± 0.70 cells. That is a real constraint on the eventual study design: the observation window has to extend well past the suspected frontier.

Chronology

When the interface existed comes back cleanly

true activity recovered

This is the strongest single result, and the one nothing in the existing boundary-detection literature offers. Mean error in the per-epoch activity is 0.00 for the always-present interface, 0.01 for the dated frontier and 0.12 for the late-appearing jurisdiction. The method recovers not just where the frontier was but that it was born and that it died.

It only works because chronology is screened jointly with geometry. Screened afterwards, a late-appearing interface has its early epochs modelled as though it already existed, the fit pushes τ toward 1 to limit the damage, and the true curve scores worse than somebody's river. That one ordering was worth 4.5 cells to 1.3.

The headline

The permeability profile says what the boundary was

recovered, mean ± sd over 5 seeds truth prices · roads · tenure · tax · settlement

Recovering a boundary's location is what wombling and change-surface models already do. Recovering which processes it stopped is not. Classified from the shape of the recovered profile alone, the joint fit calls the interface type correctly in 14 of 15 runs; the wombling baseline manages 5 of 15.

One caveat that deflates that number, and it is mine to declare. The classifier compares profile shape, so a profile with no shape — every tracer alike — lands nearest the physical template by default. On placebo data, where no interface exists at all, it called "physical" 10 times out of 10. The baseline's 5 of 15 is exactly the 5 physical runs: it called everything physical.

So the informative result is the 9 of 10 non-default calls: administrative 5/5 and economic 4/5, each distinguished from the default and from each other. The physical row is not evidence, and a real version of this needs a classifier with an explicit "no interface" class.

Kill criterion

It does invent interfaces

placebo worlds (distractors, no shared interface) real interfaces

Ten placebo worlds contain the five private discontinuities but no shared interface. The method still fits one, and the variance it explains overlaps heavily with the real cases. Only the administrative frontier separates cleanly (4 of 5 seeds above the placebo 95th percentile). The mountain interface clears it 0 times in 5; the market jurisdiction, once.

Worse for the honest version: on placebo data the method reports permeability below 0.5 — a substantial barrier — for 44% of tracers, with a median of 0.75. It does not report "nothing here". This is the project's own second kill criterion and it triggers.

What it changes

Where this leaves project 2

The prototype and the literature independently point the same way. Multi-view recovery of a shared boundary is already occupied ground — Bayesian multivariate difference boundary detection does exactly "several correlated outcomes jointly identify one boundary" — and the experiment agrees: a well-equipped wombling baseline matches the joint fit on geometry.

What is not occupied, and what survived here, is the pair the existing literature has no model for:

  • Tracer-specific permeability as a diagnostic signature. 14 of 15 against 5 of 15, and 9 of 10 on the calls that are not the classifier's default.
  • The birth and death of the interface. Activity error 0.00–0.12 across types. Wombling has no chronology at all.

So the paper is not "we localise vanished frontiers". It is "the permeability signature identifies what a vanished frontier was, and when it existed" — with localisation as machinery, not as the claim.

Before that is defensible, the calibration hole has to be filled: a null model and a detection statistic with a real false-positive rate. As it stands the method finds an interface whether or not one is there, and that is the first thing a referee will run.

Limits

What this does not establish

  • It is synthetic, and the inversion assumes the same interface family and the same PDE that generated the data. The distractors are genuinely unmodelled and the source field is generated from a Gaussian random field but fitted with 16 coarse basis functions, so the misspecification is real but mild. This is not a test on real data.
  • The interface is a single-valued curve x = c(y). Closed loops, branching frontiers and multiply-connected boundaries are untested.
  • Exactly one shared interface exists. Real regions carry several overlapping historical frontiers.
  • Permeability saturates: below τ ≈ 0.15 the field stops responding, so the data can say "nearly closed" but not how nearly. Profile shape survives this; absolute values do not.
  • Bulk conductivity is not estimable at all — with transport range far larger than the domain it is exactly degenerate with source amplitude, and fitting it biases τ. It is held fixed and absorbed into the source scale.
  • No identifiability theorem is proved. The actual paper contribution would be sufficient conditions under which independent sources and differing permeabilities recover a shared interface. This is an existence demonstration in a controlled setting, which is a weaker and different thing.
